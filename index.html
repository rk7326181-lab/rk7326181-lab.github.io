DE Rider MIS: Delivery Executive cost & productivity reporting

An automated reporting system that picks up a daily rider-data file of about 950K rows from Slack, keeps only the Delivery Executive (DE) riders, applies approved business rules and serves managers a secure dashboard. It was built entirely on free Google tools.

Problem

  • DE rider cost and productivity reporting was done by hand in Excel.
  • The daily export was 146–186 MB, with 770K–950K rows, and only about 0.6% of the rows were DE riders.
  • Every day meant filtering, lookups and recalculation, with errors and no history kept.

Approach

  • A Slack app picks up each daily file, and file-level plus row-level de-duplication means nothing is double-counted.
  • A local streaming pre-filter works around Apps Script's file-size limit by keeping only the 20 approved DE pay structures.
  • A rules engine matches the existing Excel formulas exactly, plus minimum-guarantee payouts by hub type, prorated salary, productivity, hub and zero-order flags, next-month projection and incremental burn.
  • Managers edit formulas in a sheet tab, and a metric can't go live until its formula is approved.

Impact

  • A daily manual Excel process becomes an automatic flow from Slack to dashboard.
  • Every number traces back to an approved, auditable formula.
  • All of August 2026 was backfilled: 15,054 DE rider-days.
  • Live dashboard covering 780+ DE riders and about 5 lakh orders a week, with high-DE-hub and zero-order flags.
  • It costs nothing to run: no paid hosting or database.

Cluster Payout Optimization

A geospatial cost dashboard that maps every delivery-cluster polygon and its surge rate, finds the hubs where payouts are burning money, and recommends the cheapest fix.

Problem

  • Riders earn a per-delivery surge of ₹0–₹14, set by the cluster polygon a delivery falls in.
  • Clustering incentives cost about ₹1 crore a month, and where a polygon's rate didn't match its distance from the hub, cost per order crept up.
  • Nobody could see this across 1,400+ hubs and 13,000+ polygons.

Approach

  • BigQuery pulls refreshed daily by a GitHub Actions job.
  • Haversine hub-to-polygon distances and an SOP-compliance check.
  • Shapely point-in-polygon matching of shipments to compute burn per polygon, plus a priority score.
  • "Lower the rate or widen the radius" advice with before/after cost.
  • Anomaly detection (Isolation Forest, LOF) to flag unusual hubs.

Impact

  • ₹16–17 lakh a month in savings against a ₹20 lakh-a-month reduction target.
  • One map-first view of the whole network, replacing separate Excel, My Maps and notebook steps.
  • Ranked, explainable actions for the operations team.

DA Rider Portal: Delivery Ambassador monitoring

An automated Gmail → Sheets → web-dashboard pipeline that tracks attendance, productivity, cost per order and de-hiring for Delivery Ambassador riders across four regions of India.

Problem

  • A daily attendance and productivity email was being turned into pivots by hand.
  • HR rebuilt the monthly payroll attendance file manually.
  • The old cost sheet understated real rider cost by about 60%.
  • Low-productivity and absent riders weren't followed up consistently.

Approach

  • An 8 AM job reads the mail, de-duplicates it and loads Sheets, with BigQuery for delivered and picked orders.
  • A web dashboard with ten tabs: overview, hub, POD, last 7 days, rider CPO, calling list, de-hiring, costing, attendance and fuel payout.
  • A Monday calling list, and regional de-hire drafts that a manager reviews before anything is sent.
  • The payroll workbook is generated in HR's exact format on the 1st of each month.

Impact

  • Daily reporting runs with zero manual steps.
  • Cost per order and month-end projection are visible every day instead of after month close.
  • Real rider cost is now measured correctly; the old file understated it.
  • HR's monthly attendance file is automated.

Geo Intelligence Portal (Clustering Web App)

A six-step pipeline that builds distance-based payout polygons around each hub and compares profit and loss per shipment between pincode-level and cluster-level payout.

Problem

  • Building payout polygons for hubs meant hopping between Jupyter, Google My Maps and Excel.
  • One clustering run took 4–5 hours.
  • Coverage spans 2,100+ pincodes.

Approach

  • Haversine or OSRM road distance assigns each pincode a payout slab.
  • Concentric distance rings are clipped to pincode boundaries and can be edited by dragging on a map.
  • 60 days of shipments from BigQuery are matched to polygons with a spatial index (STRtree).
  • Each hub × pincode gets a saving-vs-burning P&L, and you can ask "why is this hub burning?" in plain English.

Impact

  • 4–5 hours → 30–60 minutes per clustering run.
  • One web app replaced three tools.
  • Exports to CSV, Excel and KML for field teams.
